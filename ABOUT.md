# About This System

## What it is

The Predictive Inventory System is the day-to-day operating platform for **Steven Hydrotech Exponent Water Treatment and Supply Services**. It is where the business keeps track of what it has, what it's selling, what it needs to buy, and what it should expect to need in the future.

Before a system like this, that information tends to live in scattered places — a notebook at the counter, a spreadsheet someone updates when they remember, a supplier's delivery slip that gets filed and forgotten. This system replaces all of that with one shared, always-current source of truth that everyone in the business works from.

## Who uses it

Three kinds of people use the system, each seeing only what's relevant to their job:

- **Owners** see the whole business — every branch, every number, every user, every setting. They're the only ones who can change company-wide policy or manage other people's accounts.
- **Managers** run day-to-day operations — approving purchases, receiving deliveries, adjusting stock, running reports, and reviewing forecasts.
- **Staff** handle the hands-on work — ringing up sales, receiving goods, and making inventory corrections — but only within the specific tasks they're assigned to.

## What it's for

**Knowing what's actually in stock.** Every product has a real, trustworthy number for how much is on hand, how much is already promised to a sale or reserved, and how much is genuinely available to sell right now. That number only changes when something real happens — a sale, a delivery, a correction — never by someone quietly editing a spreadsheet cell.

**Selling at the counter.** The point-of-sale screen is built for speed: scan or search a product, build the cart, take payment, done. The moment a sale is finalized, stock drops automatically — no separate step to remember.

**Buying from suppliers.** When it's time to restock, the system tracks the whole purchasing conversation: a purchase order gets drafted, sent for approval, approved, placed with the supplier, and then checked off as deliveries arrive — even if a delivery is short or comes in more than one batch.

**Receiving deliveries correctly.** When a truck shows up, staff check what actually arrived against what was ordered — including flagging shortages, damage, or rejected items — before it counts as stock on the shelf.

**Fixing mistakes honestly.** If a stock count is wrong — damage, a miscount, spoilage — there's a proper adjustment process with a reason attached, so the correction is visible and explainable rather than a silent edit that nobody can trace later.

**Seeing trouble before it happens.** The system watches sales patterns and warns the business before a product actually runs out, so restocking happens on a schedule instead of in a panic.

**Planning ahead.** Using recent sales history, the system estimates how much of each product is likely to be needed going forward, and suggests sensible order quantities and reorder timing — treating those numbers as informed guidance for a person to decide on, never as an automatic purchase.

**Working without the internet.** Some tasks — like adjusting stock — can still be recorded even if the connection drops, for example during a delivery in an area with weak signal. Nothing is lost; it's held locally and quietly catches up with the main system the moment connectivity returns.

**Keeping an honest paper trail.** Every meaningful action — a sale, a stock change, an approval, a login — is permanently logged: who did it, when, and why. Nothing important simply disappears or gets rewritten after the fact.

**Making sense of it all.** Dashboards and reports turn the raw activity into answers to the questions that actually matter day to day: What's low? What sold this week? What's overdue from a supplier? What does next month probably look like?

## How it works, in plain terms

Everything in the system is built around one idea: **nothing about stock or money changes without leaving a trace.** A sale, a delivery, a correction, an approval — each one is a real, recorded event, not just a number quietly overwritten. That's what makes the numbers on the dashboard something people can actually trust, and what makes it possible to look back later and understand exactly how the business got to where it is.
